⚠️ Digital Harms & Youth Safety Risks

Critical Alert: Misinformation exposure affects 54-72% of South Asian youth, making it the most prevalent digital harm. This is driven by low media literacy, rapid platform migration (WhatsApp → YouTube), and algorithmic amplification of sensational content. Cyberbullying and privacy violations also show high prevalence, with Bangladesh reporting the most severe impacts across all categories.

👥 Age-Wise Internet Adoption Patterns

Youth Peak Phenomenon: All countries show maximum internet penetration in the 18-24 age group (72-85%), followed by sharp declines. The 13-17 bracket shows slightly lower adoption (52-78%), suggesting secondary school is a critical gateway. Ages 35+ show dramatically lower penetration (10-38%), indicating the digital divide remains age-stratified. This has significant implications for family-based digital literacy interventions.

💻 Device Access Inequality: The "Shared Device" Challenge

Policy Implication: An average of 58% of South Asian youth use shared family devices, compared to only 38% with personal devices. This "shared access" model fundamentally alters risk profiles: victims of cyberbullying or harassment cannot easily block harassers without family members noticing; privacy concerns about sexual/reproductive health information searches are amplified; and surveillance by family members may prevent help-seeking for exploitation. School/cyber cafe access provides a safety valve for 8-25% of youth but remains inconsistent.

🎯 Research Gaps & Policy Recommendations

Critical Data Gaps

  • Longitudinal Mental Health Data: Most prevalence data is cross-sectional. No long-term tracking of social media's mental health impact on South Asian adolescents.
  • Algorithmic Radicalization: How local-language algorithms (Hindi, Bengali, Urdu) amplify polarizing content remains largely proprietary to platforms.
  • Shared Device Behavior: How "co-viewing" on family devices changes digital risks compared to Western "personal device" models is understudied.
  • Platform Migration Effects: As users shift from Facebook → TikTok → YouTube, how does misinformation spread change? No systematic tracking.

Policy Recommendations for Governments & Civil Society

  • Digital Literacy in Schools: Integrate media literacy and online safety into national curricula, targeting ages 13-17 (peak adoption window).
  • Gender-Focused Interventions: Address the 15-26pp gender gap through subsidized access, culturally-sensitive safety training, and female digital role models.
  • Shared Device Adaptations: Design safety features for shared-device households (e.g., time-limited privacy modes, parental controls with transparency).
  • Platform Accountability: Develop regional content moderation standards and require platforms to publish transparency reports in local languages.
  • Institutional Access Expansion: Scale school and community cyber cafe programs, particularly in Bangladesh and rural areas.
